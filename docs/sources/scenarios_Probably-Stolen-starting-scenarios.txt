 Starting Scenarios Overview
 In Probably Stolen, your starting scenario — the combination of background, positive perks, and negative perks — shapes the entire opening economy of your shop. The dystopian station setting punishes poor starts harshly: rent arrives weekly, stock is scarce, and RNG-heavy days can sink an unprepared shopkeeper. Choosing the right scenario is the single most important pre-game decision.

 The scenario system works on a point budget: strong backgrounds cost more, negative perks refund points you can reinvest. A well-built scenario compensates for a weak economic start.
 Core Principle
 Match your perks to your background. If your start gives little cash, take economy perks (Extra Credits, Well Connected). If your start gives no protection, take defensive perks (Vigilant).

 Avoid Overcommitting
 Do not spend points on niche perks like specialized appraisal guides at creation. Appraisal tools appear in-game through visitors like the Appraiser, and perk points are better spent on economy and safety.
 Rock Bottom: Step-by-Step Survival
 Rock Bottom is the most punishing starting scenario — you begin with effectively nothing. The first week determines whether the run is viable. Follow this proven sequence:
 1
 Take Economy Perks
 Select Additional 150 Credits and Colorful Character. Add Minimalist as your negative perk to bank extra points, then spend them on Vigilant and Well Connected.
 2
 Buy and Flip Daily
 Accept cheap medical goods, food, and materials from early sellers. You cannot afford wholesale lots yet, so buy small, sell small, and keep money moving.
 3
 Scavenge at Night (Early Only)
 Scavenge while wounded risk is tolerable. Stop once your cash flow covers rent — wounds block future scavenging days and worsen overnight.
 4
 Secure Rent by Day 6
 Use news events (household goods surplus, water shortage) to buy cheap and sell high. Aim to hold full rent one day before it is due.
 5
 Transition to Pure Trading
 Once stable, invest in neon signs from Dr. Jackson to guarantee seller visits, then drop scavenging and appraisals for a clean buy-and-sell loop.
 Stability Checkpoint
 By the end of week two, a healthy Rock Bottom run shows estimated total wealth well above your starting credits — one documented run reached roughly 455 credits from a 150-credit start. If you are below starting wealth after week two, restart with a different perk spread.
 Key Mechanics That Interact With Your Start
 Your scenario does not operate in a vacuum. Several station mechanics either amplify or neutralize your starting choices.
 Mechanic How It Interacts Strategy 
 Wanted criminals (Odin Harlson) Reporting yields ~300 credits Strongest standard opener; skipping it defines challenge runs 
 Variety buyers 15% bonus for selling multiple item types Keep one of each cheap category in stock 
 News events Price swings on household goods, water, weapons Buy on surplus news, sell on shortage news 
 Power outages Kills machine-dependent shops Low-tech starts ignore this entirely 
 Appraisal system ~15% of item value as profit Crucial early crutch; drop it later for purity runs 
 Scavenging wounds Worsen overnight, block scavenging Never scavenge while already wounded 
 Janitorial Service
 Keep paying for janitorial service even when cutting costs. It adds customers per day, and losing store attractiveness during a fragile start compounds quickly.
 Rock Bottom Week One Checklist:
 Select Additional 150 Credits, Colorful Character, Minimalist, Vigilant 
 Flip medical goods and food within the first three days 
 Reach full rent one day before the deadline 
 Meet the Appraiser and learn at least one appraisal category 
 Decide whether Odin Harlson gets reported or protected 
 Challenge Variants and Long-Term Goals
 Once a standard Rock Bottom run feels stable, self-imposed restrictions create the real replay value. The "Rock Bottom Revolutionary" approach layers restrictions on the hardest background:
 No electricity — no machines, no power bills, live with outage events
 No reporting criminals — stay loyal to revolutionary contacts, sacrifice the easy 300-credit bounties
 No scavenging after stabilization — pure trading economy only
 No appraisals after takeoff — removes the most lucrative crutch
 Victory conditions — reach 12,000 credits (the unofficial target) or maximize revolutionary reputation
 Graduate Restrictions Gradually
 Appraising and scavenging are near-mandatory for the first two weeks of Rock Bottom. Forbid them only after your weekly cash flow comfortably exceeds rent, or the run will collapse.
 Restriction When to Apply Risk 
 No reporting From day one Low-Medium (loses 300 credits) 
 No electricity From day one Medium (blocks machines) 
 No scavenging After week two Medium (removes free stock) 
 No appraisals After week two High (removes top profit source) 
 FAQ
 Q: What is the hardest Probably Stolen starting scenario?
 Rock Bottom is the hardest scenario. You start with nothing, the opening days are highly RNG-dependent, and survival depends on flipping cheap goods and careful perk selection before the first rent payment.
 Q: Should I report Odin Harlson at the start?
 For most runs, yes. Reporting him early yields around 300 credits, which is enough to buy machinery and stabilize your economy. Only skip him in challenge runs where staying loyal to revolutionary contacts is part of the rules — note he also becomes a contraband buyer.
 Q: Which perks are worth taking on a low-cash start?
 Additional 150 Credits, Vigilant, Well Connected, and Colorful Character form the core. Take Minimalist as your negative perk because its drawback is mild and it refunds substantial perk points.
 Q: How do I survive the first rent payment on Rock Bottom?
 Flip medical supplies and food, scavenge at night while unwounded, exploit news events like household goods surpluses to buy at -40 to -60 percent, and target variety buyers for the 15 percent multi-item bonus.
